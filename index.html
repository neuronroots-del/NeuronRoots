Inside every track

The specific skills you'll actually learn

Generative AI is a stack of specific, searchable skills — not one vague subject. Here's exactly what's covered, mapped to what employers are asking for right now.

Prompt & context engineering

Writing, testing and versioning prompts that hold up in production — the foundation skill behind every GenAI Developer role.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

Connecting LLMs to real data with vector databases and retrieval pipelines — one of the most in-demand GenAI skills in 2026 job postings.

AI agents & orchestration

Building and coordinating multi-agent systems that can plan, use tools, and complete multi-step tasks.

LLM evaluation & guardrails

Measuring output quality, catching failure modes, and putting safety guardrails around production AI systems.

Model & infrastructure selection

Choosing between models, frameworks, and hosting tradeoffs — a core Generative AI Architect skill.

AI product strategy

Scoping what an AI-native product should do, and how to measure success in a probabilistic system — the core of the Generative AI PM track.

AI governance & responsible AI

Security, cost control, and governance practices required to run GenAI systems at enterprise scale.

Fine-tuning & model adaptation

Lightweight adaptation techniques for tailoring a base model to a specific use case without full retraining.

Cross-functional AI delivery

Working across engineering, design and business to ship AI features — the skill that separates an AI PM from a general PM.
